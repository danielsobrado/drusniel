## Chapter 12 | Part 2

---

At the edge of the field, the image returned.

Maris caught the fence before her knees gave way.

The same three things.

A small boat.

Black water.

A grey-dark hand losing its grip.

![Vision of boat on black water](./vision-boat-black-water_sora.jpg)

No new angle.

No missing piece restored.

No change at all.

That was what made it stable.

Not true.

Not future.

Not literal.

Stable meant only one thing: every time it returned, it returned unchanged.

Maris had learned not to grant visions more authority than they earned.

The hand slipped.

The water closed over it.

The person attached to that hand was drowning.

Or believed they were.

The vision made no distinction.

![Vision of hand reaching](./vision-hand-reaching_sora.jpg)

Maris came back against the fence with both hands locked around the wood.

Her headache sharpened.

She waited.

The road remained beside her.

The fence remained under her hands.

The sun remained low.

"Fine," she said.

Nothing answered.

She had seen deaths before they happened.

She had seen arguments that never happened at all.

She had once watched a house burn three times and later discovered that the owner had only dreamed of fire.

Visions were not records.

They were experiences without instructions.

This one was simply harder to shake.

She pressed her thumbnail into the scar on her palm.

Here.

Fence.

Dust.

Evening.

The hand returned.

Still the same.

![Maris wakes gasping](./maris-wakes-gasping_sora.jpg)

"Very helpful."

Her own bitterness sounded almost normal.

That helped too.

She pushed away from the fence and kept walking.

![Maris walks on](./maris-walks-on_sora.jpg)

Whatever the image was, Riverhold still lay ahead.

And the closer she came to the town, the worse the pressure behind her eyes became.

That, at least, was measurable.
