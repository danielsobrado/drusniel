## Chapter 13 | Part 1

---

Seven guards. Four wagons. Eleven prisoners. Three cages.

Drusniel repeated the numbers until the drugged heaviness behind his eyes stopped mattering.

The caravan had halted at a waypoint outpost on the eastern route. They kept him in a stone room apart from the other prisoners, fed him twice a day, and put something in both food and water.

Not enough to make him unconscious.

Enough to make standing expensive.

Three days since Merrik sold him.

His magic remained a hollow ache.

The Null rested beneath his shirt.

The guards had searched him twice. One had pulled the pouch free, opened it, weighed the metal in his palm and found nothing worth fearing. Merrik's instruction had followed with the sale: crossing survivors fetched more when they arrived with what they had carried through.

So they left it.

Cold metal.

Inert, as far as they could tell.

Useful mistake.

![The caravan through a hostile landscape](./caravan-through-wrong-landscape_sora.jpg)

Beyond the shutter: black sand, crystal formations, volcanic light.

Inside: rough floorboards, narrow bed, stone wall.

One hundred and forty-seven scratches in the wooden doorframe.

![Cracks in the cage bars](./cracks-in-the-bars_sora.jpg)

Drusniel had traced them twice.

Then he traced the floorboards.

Then the seams in the stone.

When there was nothing left to count, he watched the prisoner in the far cage.

![The creature watches from the far cage](./the-creature-watches_sora.jpg)

Grey skin.

Red markings across face and arms.

Long features that suggested elven ancestry without matching it.

The guards called him demon-possessed.

The other prisoners avoided looking at him.

He looked back at Drusniel.

Not constantly.

At useful moments.

When guards changed.

When food arrived.

When a wagon moved.

Drusniel stopped thinking of the watching as accidental.

A wagon hit a rut while the yard crew repositioned it. The far cage swung hard against its restraints.

The prisoner's lips twitched.

Drusniel almost returned it.

Almost.

![The other prisoners avoid him](./other-prisoners-avoid-him_sora.jpg)

From guard conversations he had learned that twenty-three prisoners had already been sold.

The eastern markets were three days away.

That left time.

His body was weak.

His magic was worse.

He had no weapon.

But time was still a resource.

Before the light changed, two guards came for him.

They marched him from the room and pushed him into the second cage on the nearest wagon.

The stone room disappeared behind him.

So did the doorframe.

One hundred and forty-seven scratches.

The cage was smaller.

Iron bars.

Splintered floor.

Canvas overhead.

But from it he could still see the grey prisoner in the far cage.

Watching.

The wagon lurched forward.

Drusniel counted the guards again.

Seven.

Still seven.
